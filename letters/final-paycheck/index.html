Ask for your final paycheck

Pick your state and how the job ended. The letter fills in, with the deadline where the law gives a day count.

Type your details

What you type stays on this page. Nothing is sent or saved.

Sample dataLetter to your employer
Pay due by19 March 2026
Saturday 14 March 2026 Acme Supply Co., 1 Main Street, Austin, TX 78701 Re: Final pay To whom it may concern: My last day of work was Friday 13 March 2026. I am asking about my final pay. Please send me, in writing: 1. The date my final wages will be paid. 2. A statement of the hours or pay period covered and the amount of the final wages. I understand that Texas Labor Code section 61.014 provides that an employer is to pay in full an employee who is discharged not later than the sixth day after the date of discharge. I understand the statute also has other conditions and exceptions. Please send the statement and any payment to the address below. Sincerely, Sam Ortiz 88 Pecan Lane, Austin, TX 78704

This is a sample with made-up details. Type yours and the blanks fill in.

Sample: Texas: the sixth day after discharge, by this site's count: Thursday 19 March 2026.

What to attach

  • Your last pay stub.
  • The message or letter that ended the job, if you have one.
  • Your record of hours or days worked in the last pay period.
  • Your mailing address, in writing (the letter has it).

Where to send it

Send it to the employer's payroll or HR contact in a way that gives you a receipt, such as certified mail or an email you keep. Keep a copy.

The rule behind this letter

The rule, in short: six states, six rules. Some give a day count, some say the next payday, some say immediately. Read on 14 November 2026. This site's reading.

StateDeadline in the statuteSource
CaliforniaDischarged: immediately, with some exceptions (for example seasonal layoffs and state employers). Quit, for an employee without a written contract for a definite period: not later than 72 hours after quitting, or at the time of quitting if 72 hours of notice was given.California Labor Code §§201 and 202
MichiganDischarged: immediately, as soon as the amount can with due diligence be determined. Quit: as soon as the amount can with due diligence be determined. The statute sets different rules for some contract employees and hand-harvest workers.Michigan Compiled Laws §408.475
New YorkNot later than the regular pay day for the pay period during which the termination occurred.New York Labor Law §191
North CarolinaOn or before the next regular payday.North Carolina General Statutes §95-25.7
TexasDischarged: not later than the sixth day after discharge. Left other than by discharge: not later than the next regularly scheduled payday.Texas Labor Code §61.014
WashingtonAt the end of the established pay period, with exceptions for some multi-employer industry plans and some labor-management agreements.Revised Code of Washington 49.48.010

The sample uses Texas. Its rule, word for word:

“An employer shall pay in full an employee who is discharged from employment not later than the sixth day after the date the employee is discharged.”

Texas Labor Code §61.014, official text, read 14 November 2026.
