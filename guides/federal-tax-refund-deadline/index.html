Federal tax refund deadline: the 3-year and 2-year rule

A refund you never claimed does not wait forever. The IRS sets a time limit. This page quotes the rule, names some exceptions the IRS lists and works out your date in your browser.

Start here. A federal income tax refund has a time limit. The IRS says it is generally the later of 3 years from when you filed or 2 years from when you paid the tax. If a refund is still unclaimed, find your date below.

This site cannot look up your refund or file anything. It is information, not legal advice or tax advice. A tax professional or the IRS can tell you what applies to you.

The rule

The IRS calls the deadline the Refund Statute Expiration Date.

The latest date, by law, you can claim a credit or federal income tax refund for a specific tax year is generally the later of these 2 dates:

3 years from the date you filed your federal income tax return, or

2 years from the date you paid the tax.

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

“If you filed your return before its due date, the IRS considers it filed on the due date. If you had income tax withheld or paid estimated tax during the year, we consider those payments to have been made on the return due date.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

This site's reading: tax withheld during the year counts as paid on the return due date, so for a return filed on time the three years from filing is usually the later date. If you paid tax at some later time, the two years from that payment can be later.

If you miss it

“You can't get a credit or refund if you don't file the claim within 3 years of filing your original return, or 2 years after paying the tax, whichever is later, unless you meet an exception that allows you more time to file a claim.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

The IRS also limits how much you can get, depending on which date you use.

“Your credit or refund is limited to the amount you paid during the 3 years before you filed the claim, plus any extensions of time you had to file your return.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

“Your credit or refund is limited to the amount you paid within the 2 years right before you filed your claim.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

The IRS lists some exceptions.

“Are affected by a Presidentially declared disaster: You may have up to 1 more year to claim a credit or refund.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

The IRS page also names exceptions for a written agreement with the IRS, for service in a combat zone and for bad debt deductions, and its other page lists more. This site did not summarise them.

Work out the later date

This tool runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere. It does the arithmetic of the two dates in the IRS rule above. The IRS decides what applies to you.

If you filed before the due date, enter the due date, as the IRS says above. The due date is the April tax deadline.

Tax withheld or estimated tax counts as paid on the return due date, as the IRS says above.

How to file a claim

The IRS says how to file a claim, on an original return or on an amended return.

“Generally, to claim a refund, you must file an amended return within 3 years after the date you filed your original return or 2 years after the date you paid the tax, whichever is later. If you filed early, count from the April tax deadline.”

IRS, File an amended return, read 14 November 2026.

“Claim a credit or refund for other taxes (not income taxes) and penalties on Form 843, Claim for Refund and Request for Abatement.”

IRS, Time you can claim a credit or refund, read 14 November 2026.

Where to check a refund you already filed for: IRS refund status page, which also covers amended returns.
