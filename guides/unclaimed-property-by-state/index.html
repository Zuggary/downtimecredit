Unclaimed property: where to search in ten large states

States hold money from forgotten bank accounts, uncashed checks and more. Each state runs its own search. This page links the official search for ten large states and quotes what each says.

Start here. Pick your state below, then use the state's own official search. Searching is the state's service, not this site's.

This site cannot look up a person's property and does not see what you type at a state site. Each state decides every claim. This page is information, not legal advice.

Covers California, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Michigan, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Texas. State missing? This site has not checked it yet. Search your state treasurer's or comptroller's official website for unclaimed property.

Before you search

  • Use only the state's official site, reached from the links on this page or by typing the address. Texas says it will never text or call asking for banking credentials, and Florida says it will not contact you by text about your claim.
  • This site's suggestion: search your name, your maiden or former names, your business names and your old addresses.
  • This site's suggestion: also search for relatives who have died. Some states, such as Ohio and New York, have pages for heirs.

California

Open the California official search →

California State Controller, ClaimIt

California's search is run by the State Controller. The home page says there is no deadline. It also describes a kind of notice the state sends to some owners.

“There's no deadline to claim your property, and the process is free.”

California State Controller, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“If you received an official notice from the California Unclaimed Property Division of the State Controller's Office —by mail or email—that includes a Claim ID you did not initiate, it is legitimate and part of our expedited pay process to reunite owners with unclaimed property.”

California State Controller, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“No property search is needed; instead, use the secure link provided in the notice to complete your claim.”

California State Controller, official page, read 14 November 2026.

This site's caution: check that a notice really came from the State Controller before you click a link in it. If you are unsure, open the state's site yourself from the link above. This site read only the home page of the search.

New York's Comptroller runs the program.

“After a period of inactivity, lost money is legally considered abandoned property and turned over to the New York State Comptroller’s Office, where there’s no fee or time limit to claim it.”

Ohio

Open the Ohio official search →

Ohio Department of Commerce, Unclaimed Funds

Ohio has a time limit. A 2025 law set it. The Division of Unclaimed Funds explains it on a page about claiming.

“As a result of HB96, signed into law in 2025, Ohioans now have 10 years to claim any unclaimed funds reported to the Division.”

Ohio Department of Commerce, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“All funds reported before Jan. 1, 2026 may be claimed through Jan. 1, 2036.”

Ohio Department of Commerce, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“After Jan. 1, 2026, claims will only be accepted within ten years of when the Division first received the funds.”

Ohio Department of Commerce, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“If funds aren’t claimed within that timeframe, they become property of the state and can no longer be claimed.”

Ohio Department of Commerce, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“You don't have to work with a paid professional finder, as you can claim your money for free on our website.”

Ohio Department of Commerce, official page, read 14 November 2026.

This site's reading: in Ohio, do not wait. Search now, and claim directly with the state.

Texas

Open the Texas official search →

Texas Comptroller, Claim It Texas

Texas's Comptroller runs the program. Its home page warns about scams and says how payments are made.

“SCAM ALERT: The Comptroller’s office will never text or call requesting your online banking credentials, secure access code or any other personally identifiable information for any reason.”

Texas Comptroller, official page, read 14 November 2026.

“Unclaimed property payments are issued via physical check sent through standard USPS mail; direct deposits and wire transfers are not available.”

Texas Comptroller, official page, read 14 November 2026.
